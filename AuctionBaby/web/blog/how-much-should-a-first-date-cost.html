How much should a first date cost?

Wrong question — but a useful one. The honest answer is that the date should cost whatever the plan costs, stated before anyone walks in. Ambiguity is expensive; clarity is free.

The three rules that actually matter

1. Price the plan, not the person. A bigger number doesn't buy a better evening — a better-fitting plan does.

2. Say the number early. "I'm thinking a $60 taco-and-vinyl night" filters better than any bio.

3. A budget is a commitment to the date, never a payment to a person. That distinction is the whole game.

Where Auction Baby fits

It's a dating app built on exactly those rules: men bid a committed date budget, women pick the plan — and person — they like. Highest bid doesn't win; the best-matched plan does. No money moves in the app, ever.
