Dating app red flags for women

Ask any woman who has dated online for six months and you'll get the same list. The patterns are so universal they have names — which tells you the problem isn't the men, it's the structure that lets the behavior happen at zero cost.

The big four

  • Endless chat, no plan. Weeks of "hey beautiful" weather-talk that never becomes a Tuesday. The chat itself is the entertainment; you were never the destination.
  • Low-effort openers. "Hey." "What's up?" If the opener costs nothing, the next four hours of your life are priced accordingly.
  • Bait-and-switch. The profile says dinner and cocktails. The date is "come over, I'll cook."
  • The vanishing act. A plan exists, then dissolves an hour before, no consequence, no cost.

Why every app has them

Every major app makes those behaviors free. Infinite matching, infinite chatting, infinite flaking — all at zero cost to the person doing them. When effort is free, low effort is the rational play. The apps didn't attract the wrong people; they priced the right behavior out of the market.

The structural fix

Auction Baby changes what's on the table — literally. Before any date exists, a man commits a date budget: the real plan and what it costs to make it happen. She picks the plan — and the person — she likes. Never the highest bid; the best-fitting one. The money never moves inside the app: a bid is a promise, not a payment.

The red flags survive only where they're free. Where a plan and a commitment are required up front, the endless-chat crowd doesn't bid at all — which is exactly the filter working.
