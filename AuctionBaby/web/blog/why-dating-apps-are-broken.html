Why dating apps are broken

Not because of algorithms, and not because of any gender war. Dating apps are broken the way a casino is "broken" — they're working exactly as designed. A swipe app's revenue is your attention. The day you meet someone great is the day you stop opening the app. Every design decision follows from that fact.

Three design choices that guarantee bad dates

1. Infinite supply. An endless carousel of faces means every match is replaceable in one swipe. Replaceability is the enemy of effort — nothing needs to work out.

2. Zero-cost conversation. Chat that costs nothing produces nothing. Six weeks of "hey" is rational behavior when the alternative — proposing an actual plan — carries all the risk and none of the reward.

3. No commitment surface. There is nowhere in a swipe app that says "here is a real plan, on a real night, and I'm putting something on it." So nobody does.

The scarcity fix

Auction Baby runs the opposite design. The floor has five dates a night, and bids close at midnight. Men bid a real date budget — a committed plan, priced. Women field a small number of real offers instead of an infinite queue of openers, and pick the one they actually like. She never sees the highest bidder unless she wants to. No money moves in the app — a bid is a promise, not a payment.

Scarcity is what prices effort back into dating. When bids are limited, everyone brings their best plan instead of their worst opener.
