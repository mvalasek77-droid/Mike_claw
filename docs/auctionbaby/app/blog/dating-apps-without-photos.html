Dating apps without photos

Every swipe-based app optimizes for the same thing: the fastest possible judgment of a face. The result is well documented — a small share of users receive nearly all the attention, everyone else sends messages into the void, and the actual date (the point of the whole exercise) gets planned last, if at all.

What photo-first gets wrong

Photos measure one thing. They don't measure whether the date will be good. They don't measure effort, planning, or how someone treats you when the plan goes sideways. And because everyone is judged on the same axis, the apps quietly become a leaderboard — with 95% of people losing.

Inverting the funnel

Auction Baby shows the plan before the person. Men bid a committed date budget — what they'd actually spend on making a great evening happen. Women browse the bids: a gallery night, a taco crawl, a jazz bar, a $350 tasting menu. She opens the one she likes. The photo comes after the pick, not before it.

The bid is a promise, not a payment — no money moves in the app, ever. What's being evaluated is initiative, not income.

Who this is for

Women tired of being a swiping queue. Men who are terrible in photos but great in person. Anyone who's ever thought "I'd rather have one good Tuesday night than 400 matches."
